About the Showrunner / Profile 2026

Dan
Pradana [MXVDXN]

CopywriterCreatorJakarta10+ Years in APAC Advertising

Dan Pradana is a Jakarta-based copywriter and the creator of MXVDXN, an independent collection of original fiction, strange characters and interconnected worlds made with generative AI.

His stories tend to find their way into the less polished corners of Southeast Asia. The crowded kos-kosan, narrow Jakarta alleys, roadside food stalls, cheap apartments, motorbike crews and fluorescent convenience stores. Places that feel familiar, a little chaotic and full of people with their own problems.

There is a particular affection for the absurdity of everyday life. Ordinary conversations can suddenly turn into elaborate conspiracies, supernatural encounters or ridiculous acts of violence. And sometimes, nothing particularly dramatic happens at all.

That unpredictability is part of the appeal.

01 /Practice

Dan is currently a copywriter at a global payments technology company, following a career in advertising agencies spanning more than a decade across Asia Pacific.

His professional work has taken him through different industries, from food and beverage, automotive and telecommunications to financial technology and lifestyle. Years of working with creative teams, demanding briefs and tight deadlines have shaped his approach to ideas, storytelling and execution.

But his creative interests have always extended beyond advertising.

Cinema, music, Japanese subcultures, Southeast Asian street life and underground media have long occupied another part of his imagination. MXVDXN is where those influences come together without the expectations of a client brief.

Generative AI has given him a new way to work with those ideas, allowing him to develop original characters, direct scenes and build fictional worlds independently.

What began as visual experiments has gradually grown into an expanding collection of stories, with recurring characters, ongoing narratives and worlds that occasionally collide.

02 /Visual Language

There are certain faces, places and personalities that keep appearing throughout Dan's work.

Girls with braces. Dyed hair. Cheap accessories. School uniforms. Motorbikes. A particular kind of street attitude that exists somewhere between Jakarta's cabe-cabean culture and Bangkok's skoi scene.

Both subcultures have their own histories and social baggage, often dismissed as tacky, loud or troublesome. Dan is drawn to their visual identity, humor, contradictions and unapologetic presence.

His female characters are rarely interested in behaving the way people expect. They can be vain, funny, vulgar, affectionate, reckless or surprisingly thoughtful. Sometimes they save the day. Sometimes they make everything worse.

His camera language has a similar fascination with imperfection.

Shaky handheld footage, unusual framing, awkward close-ups, fluorescent lighting and the occasional accidental beauty of an ordinary street corner. There is a strong affection for the immediacy of amateur video, Japanese independent cinema and the visual noise of Southeast Asian cities.

Pretty ones bite.

03 /Fictional Worlds

AI Micro Series

SARI & PLOY brings together many of Dan's recurring interests: Southeast Asian school culture, secret agents, martial arts, friendship, fashion and the occasional spectacularly bad decision.

Hideaki Anno's Love & Pop is a major influence on its visual language, while the playful glamour of Charlie's Angels finds its way into the characters and action. Silat, Muay Thai and Southeast Asian street culture give the series its own identity.

What started as a relatively small experiment has grown into a shared fictional universe, with characters and stories extending into LARASATI and other interconnected narratives.

Elsewhere, 9 NAILS explores the uneasy coexistence of ordinary people and supernatural beings, while BAD GOLD moves into a different world of crime, power and complicated relationships.

Not everything is about fighting, though.

Dan also has a fondness for quieter stories, particularly those about unlikely friendships, everyday kindness and people discovering something unexpected in one another.

The short format suits his way of working. Each episode offers room to explore a character, an atmosphere or a moment without forcing every idea into a conventional structure.

As the technology develops, so does the process. Experiments become scenes, scenes suggest new stories and occasionally a minor character becomes more interesting than originally intended.

04 /MXVDXN

There is no single genre that neatly describes MXVDXN.

Some stories are sensual and violent. Others are ridiculous, melancholic or unexpectedly sweet. Beautiful people share the screen with strange creatures, rundown buildings and situations that probably shouldn't be happening in the first place.

The common thread is Dan's affection for characters with personality and for Southeast Asia as a place that is far more interesting than its usual postcard image.

MXVDXN sits somewhere between independent cinema, underground culture magazines and a growing archive of personal obsessions.

It's a place for stories that don't necessarily belong anywhere else.

And perhaps that's exactly the point.
